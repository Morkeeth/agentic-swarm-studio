1The problem

You know the route through your app. A new visitor does not.

Give the simulator one URL and one job. A small cast of model visitors opens the app in separate browsers and tries to finish the job. You see what each visitor read, clicked, missed, and claimed. A second model checks the screen evidence. The result keeps failures visible instead of turning them into a polished score.

2Who it is for

A release check for teams that ship fast.

  1. Founder shipping an agent-built app. Check whether a fresh visitor can complete the first job before you send traffic.
  2. Agency handing an app to a client. Give the client a replay and a repeatable acceptance check they can inspect.
  3. Product team changing an agent flow. Compare the same goal before and after a model, prompt, interface, or tool change.

3Method

From one job to evidence you can inspect.

  1. 3.1 Set the job

    Enter a URL and one plain task. The current public service shows recorded work. New paid runs stay local and require an explicit model key and spending stop.

  2. 3.2 Send the swarm

    Up to five NVIDIA Nemotron Nano visitors open separate Chromium sessions. One uses a phone viewport. They can read, click, scroll, and go back. They cannot type, sign in, pay, upload, or save.

  3. 3.3 Keep every path

    The simulator saves the page text, available controls, chosen action, browser response, and screenshot at each step. Each colour on the map is one recorded visitor.

  4. 3.4 Check the claim

    When a visitor says the job is complete, Nemotron 3.5 Super VL checks the final screen image and its text. Local deterministic rules also check that the required moves happened.

  5. 3.5 Return a repeatable check

    A confirmed error path can become a small browser regression test. Unconfirmed claims, model failures, and unfinished visits remain visible as limits.

  6. 3.6 Run the same job after a fix

    Compare the exact journey before and after the change. The longer-term goal is to test the same job with consenting people and measure where the simulation was wrong.

4Models and infrastructure

Two model roles, one inspectable handoff.

NVIDIA parts used: both Nemotron models run through Nebius Token Factory's OpenAI-compatible inference service. Chromium runs on the application host. Local task rules, evidence checks, cost reservations, and saved replays are our code. On the public Cloud Run service, the Ask Nemotron button in the studio makes one live Nemotron Nano call through Token Factory to explain a recorded run. Calls are rate-limited and capped, and each one is logged with its tokens and cost. The hosted page does not start new swarms, and no visitor text reaches a model.

6Live versus simulated

What this public build does today.

6.1 Live now

  • This landing page and the complete recorded studio
  • saved browser swarms with real screenshots and model-call records [1]
  • Ten cached before and after code cases with fixed checkers [2]
  • A 200-site, read-only first-visit evidence library [3]
  • Replay, path inspection, and test download with no new model call

6.2 Simulated or not yet live

  • The visitors are models, not people or demographic evidence
  • The hosted service cannot start a new swarm
  • The latest working-guide run completed 0 of 5 tasks [1]
  • Human comparison is 0 of 30 planned labels [4]
  • No live checkout, paid human test, customer sale, or validated revenue forecast

7Limits

What the recorded results can and cannot show.

  1. Test visitors are an NVIDIA model reading and clicking your pages. They are not people, and nothing here is a quote from a real user. [1]
  2. They only read. Nothing was typed, no account was used, and anything behind sign-in was left untested. [1]
  3. Three states are kept apart. Claimed: the visitor said the job was done. Screen confirmed: a second call, given the steps and the text of that screen, agreed and pointed at words on it. Job completed: the steps also show every part the task names. The model calls can be wrong. [1]
  4. The ten code cases are command-line tool fixes on historical CPython code, not browser app fixes. Their gains say nothing yet about browser visitors or people. [2]
  5. A confirmed completion has been recorded on a real app: in the PuppyTime replay, 2 of 3 visitors opened the Donate page and the judge confirmed both. It is one app, so it shows the path works, not how often. [1]
  6. The first-visit study is one saved visit per app, timed once on one network. It is not a speed ranking. [3]
